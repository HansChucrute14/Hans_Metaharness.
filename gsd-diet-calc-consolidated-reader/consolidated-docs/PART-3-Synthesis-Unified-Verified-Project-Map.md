## §1. The Verdict in One Sentence

> **Today the system can return `SAFE_TO_FEED` for a diet with a Ca:Mg ratio 631% out of range, with no way for the user to perceive this.**

That single sentence is the bottom-line verdict of the entire consolidated analysis, and it does not require any qualification. It binds together the two gravest defects the project exhibits today, and it is the reason — emphatically, not a rhetorical caution — that no diet generated by the current code should be fed to any dog. This is the real state of the code at commit `c932a21` on 2026-07-25, not a hypothetical risk or a "potential issue."

The two defects the sentence fuses are:

1. **Fake output (A3, the reporting layer).** The `nutrient_results` block that the solver emits — the very block a user would read to decide whether a diet is balanced — is hardcoded to `"adequate"` for every nutrient, every time. The `pct_of_min` and `pct_of_sul` fields are always `null`. The block exists only as a name; it does not report anything that the solver actually computed. Direct execution of `solve_cascade()` against two real ingredient selections confirmed this concretely: for `arginine_g`, the live output is `value=0`, `status="adequate"`, `target_min=None`. The status claims success for a nutrient the report itself shows as zero with no target. The A3 finding is documented in Part 1 §A.A3 (LP-F5); its remediation is Task B1, the headline act of Part 2 §5.2 (Execution Phase 1).

2. **A "hard" constraint that is actually soft (A2, the antagonism layer).** The mineral-antagonism constraints (calcium-magnesium, calcium-zinc, calcium-iron, calcium-phosphorus, copper-zinc, and so on) are declared `HARD_FAIL_INFEASIBLE` in `data/constraints.json` — five mineral-antagonism constraints (Ca:P, Zn:Cu, Fe:Zn, Ca:Mg, Lys:Arg), out of sixty total `HARD_FAIL_INFEASIBLE` declarations in the file, confirmed by direct grep against the live file. But the solver does not enforce them as infeasibility. They are implemented as slack variables with a penalty in the objective, which means the solver can return a feasible solution that violates them and simply absorbs the penalty. The "hard" contract exists in the configuration; it does not exist in the solver behavior. A diet that is 631% out of range on Ca:Mg is therefore not only possible — it is silently emitted as `SAFE_TO_FEED`. This is the A2 finding (Part 1 §A.A2 / LP-F2), and its remediation is Task B2a (harden antagonisms at Level 1, Part 2 §9.3) plus Task B2b (severity-scaled recommendation, Part 2 §9.3).

The 631% figure is not arbitrary. It is the worst-case observed excursion: the most extreme Ca:Mg imbalance that the solver's penalty mechanism allows to be silently accepted. Combined with the A3 reporting layer that always says "adequate," this means the worst possible outcome — a diet that could produce clinically significant electrolyte disturbance in a growing large-breed German Shepherd — is indistinguishable in the output from a perfectly balanced one. There is no warning, no flag, no degraded recommendation, no indication anywhere in the user-facing surface that anything is wrong.

The reason this sentence matters more than any other in the consolidated documentation is that it forces a clean priority order on the remediation. The two defects are not independent: they are the two ends of the same safety failure. Fixing only the output layer (B1) would tell the user the truth, but the solver would still produce dangerous diets; fixing only the antagonism layer (B2a) would make the solver safe, but the user would still see "adequate" and have no way to distinguish a clean optimum from a violated one. Both must be done, and they must be done in the same execution window — which is exactly why they anchor the Blockers and Safety-Chain phases of the unified map (§5 below).

## §3. Structural Patterns vs. Surgical Fixes

The single most useful distinction in the entire consolidated analysis is the one between **structural defects** (architectural patterns that repeat across independent parts of the codebase and re-emerge whenever a new feature is added) and **surgical defects** (point failures, even severe ones, that can be corrected by a localized change). The distinction matters because it changes the remediation strategy: surgical defects are fixed one at a time, structural defects require the introduction of a design principle that the codebase does not currently have. Confusing the two — treating a structural defect as a one-off bug, or treating a surgical defect as a redesign — wastes effort and produces fragile results.

### §3.1 The Structural Pattern: "Contracts That Exist in Name Only"

The single most important cross-cutting finding of the diagnosis is that the codebase systematically declares contracts it does not enforce. The pattern surfaces in at least four independent places, and the repetition is the signal that this is not four bugs but one missing design principle:

1. **The output contract is fictional.** `nutrient_results` is documented as the solver's report of what each nutrient actually came out to. In reality, every entry is hardcoded `"adequate"` with null percentages. The contract exists in the function signature and the docstring; it does not exist in the function body. (A3; Part 1 §A.A3 / LP-F5.)
2. **The constraint contract is fictional.** Five mineral-antagonism constraints are declared `HARD_FAIL_INFEASIBLE` in `data/constraints.json` (out of sixty total `HARD_FAIL_INFEASIBLE` declarations; the other fifty-five are nutrient minimums and toxicological limits), confirmed by direct grep against the live file. The solver treats every one of them as a slack-penalized soft constraint. The contract exists in the config; it does not exist in the LP that the solver builds. (A2; Part 1 §A.A2 / LP-F2.)
3. **The schema contract is fictional.** The 44 KB `lp_parameters.schema.json` is presented as the canonical schema for the LP parameters data file. It validates zero real files: the data file it nominally governs does not match it (3 errors at the top level — fields like `breed` and `domains` that the schema expects but the data does not contain, and vice versa). The contract exists on disk; it does not exist in any CI gate or runtime check. (C4; Part 1 §A.C4 / DATA-F4.)
4. **The self-conformance contract is fictional.** The ingredient bank (`lp_parameters_data.json`) does not validate against its own governing schema — twenty-one errors when `jsonschema.Draft202012Validator` is run against it. There is no CI gate that catches this. The data file advertises itself as schema-conformant; the schema says it is not; nothing in the pipeline connects the two facts. (C1; Part 1 §A.C1 / DATA-F1.)

This is **not four bugs; it is one missing design principle.** The project does not have a mechanism that forces "what the code says it does" to match "what the code actually does." The fix is not to patch each of the four instances — it is to introduce the principle of **verified contracts**: every declared contract (output, constraint, schema, self-conformance) must be backed by a check that fails the build when the declaration and the behavior diverge. The four instances are then fixed as a consequence of the principle, not as four separate items. This is the deep structural remediation that Tasks B6 (CI schema gate), B7 (canonical nutrient registry with schema enforcement), C5/C9 (schema hardening), and B1 (real reporting layer) collectively implement — see Part 2 §5.2, §6, and §9.

### §3.2 The Structural Pattern: The Fragmented Nutrient Namespace

The second structural defect is a data-model problem, not a code bug. The codebase has **three competing nutrient-naming schemes** that coexist without a canonical reference:

- The **FDC scheme** (USDA FoodData Central), used by the validation pipeline's FDC fetcher.
- The **COFID scheme** (UK CoFID table), used by the CoFID fetcher.
- The **solver/bank scheme** (`arginine_g`, `calcium_mg`, `phosphorus_mg`, and so on), used by the LP matrix builder and the ingredient bank.

These three schemes have **zero overlap** in key naming. The mapping between them is implicit and scattered across the codebase — there is no single registry that says "this FDC nutrient corresponds to this COFID nutrient corresponds to this solver nutrient." A typo in any one of the three schemes (`argnine_g` instead of `arginine_g`) is invisible to the schema because the schema has no enumeration of valid nutrient keys — the schema is blind to key-set errors (Part 1 §A.C2 / DATA-F2). This is the closest the project comes to needing genuine redesign rather than remediation: the right fix is a single canonical registry (`id + unit + basis`) that every other module references by ID, and that the schemas enforce. This is Task B7 (Part 2 §5.1, P0-5), and it is correctly sequenced as a slower, high-value structural task rather than a blocker — the surgical fixes can land without it, but every surgical fix is more fragile until B7 lands.

### §3.3 The Structural Pattern: Level-1 Unreachability

The third structural defect is that **Level 1 (`SAFE_TO_FEED`) is structurally unreachable.** The lexicographic cascade has three levels: Level 1 (canonical optimum, all hard constraints satisfied, no antagonism violation), Level 2 (relaxed — antagonism slack permitted with penalty), Level 3 (further relaxed — inclusion constraints relaxed via boolean flag). Direct execution of the cascade against two real selections — a 5-ingredient reference selection and a 10-ingredient broad selection — produced the same result both times: `cascade_level=2`, `solver_status="suboptimal"`. Level 1 was never reached.

This was originally observed in five test selections (5 selections × 2 scenarios = 10 runs, all stopped at Level 2) during the empirical amendment. The direct re-execution against the live repository confirmed it with two further selections. The pattern is therefore not an artifact of a specific test fixture; it is a property of the cascade as currently configured. The cause is not yet diagnosed — it could be a single mis-calibrated constraint (an overly tight minimum, an overly strict antagonism bound) or something deeper in the lexicographic ordering itself. This is the largest open structural unknown in the project, and it is the reason Task B11 (diagnose Level-1 infeasibility via IIS / constraint relaxation analysis) exists (Part 2 §9.4).

The practical consequence is severe: because Level 1 is unreachable, **the only thing that protects the dog today is whatever the lower levels say.** Level 2 permits antagonism violations; Level 3 relaxes inclusion constraints. If the recommendation engine (B2b, severity-scaled feeding recommendation) is not implemented — and at commit `c932a21` it is not — then there is no mechanism in the codebase that distinguishes "this diet is fine" from "this diet is 631% out of range on a critical mineral ratio." Both are emitted with the same `SAFE_TO_FEED` label. This is why Task B2b, not Task B2a, is described as "the decisive protection" in §5 below: B2a makes Level 1 honest, but Level 1 is unreachable; B2b makes Level 2 honest, and Level 2 is what the user actually receives.

### §3.4 The Surgical Defects (Critical, But Localized)

The surgical defects are no less severe — several are safety-critical — but they are point failures with point fixes:

| Surgical defect | Fix | Task |
|---|---|---|
| No absolute calcium ceiling in the LP (Large-Breed-Growth developmental-orthopedic-disease safeguard missing) | Add an absolute Ca upper-bound constraint to the LP matrix. | B3 (Part 2 §5.2) |
| Growth energy multiplier `k = 1.2 × RER` is flat, age-independent, and the scenario labels are inverted | Replace the flat multiplier with an age-banded table; correct the inverted labels. | B4 (Part 2 §5.2) |

Each of these is a localized fix. None of them requires redesigning a subsystem. They are safety-critical — the missing calcium ceiling alone is the difference between a diet that protects a growing large-breed dog's orthopedic development and one that does not — but they are not architectural. They can land in a single execution window, and the unified map (§5) places them there.

The distinction matters because it tells you where to spend architectural effort and where not to. The structural defects (§3.1–§3.3) need a design principle (verified contracts, canonical registry, Level-1 diagnosis); the surgical defects need a competent engineer with a few afternoons. Spending redesign effort on the surgical list would be malpractice; treating the structural list as a set of one-off patches would guarantee recurrence. The unified map respects this distinction by placing structural tasks (B7, B12, C5/C9) in their own slower-but-higher-value lane (§5.4), separate from the surgical blockers (§5.1) and the safety chain (§5.2).

## §4. Does Anything Need Rewriting from Scratch?

**No.** This is one of the most important conclusions of the entire consolidated analysis, and it is worth being explicit about: the mathematical core of the system is correct, and it should be preserved.

The verified core consists of:

- The **lexicographic cascade** (three-level priority ordering: Level 1 canonical optimum → Level 2 antagonism-relaxed → Level 3 inclusion-relaxed), which correctly expresses the design intent that hard-constraint satisfaction dominates soft-constraint satisfaction, which dominates inclusion goals.
- The **fix-optimum mechanism** (each level fixes the previous level's optimum as a bound before relaxing), which correctly prevents later levels from producing solutions strictly worse than earlier levels.
- The **per-ingredient Big-M** formulation, which correctly models the discrete inclusion/exclusion of each ingredient in the diet.
- The **normalized-deviation** objective, which correctly weights nutrient shortfalls and excesses on a comparable scale.
- The **Resting Energy Requirement (RER)** calculation, the standard veterinary formula.
- The **Modified Atwater** energy calculation (the canine-appropriate metabolizable-energy formula, distinct from the Atwater general factors used for human food), correctly applied.
- The **AAFCO per-1000 kcal** normalization, which correctly expresses nutrient requirements on an energy-density basis rather than a dry-matter or as-fed basis, matching the AAFCO 2024 dog food nutrient profiles.

These were verified both by the five-reviewer adversarial diagnosis and by direct PuLP 3.3.2 execution against the live repository. They are not the source of any defect cataloged in Part 1, and they are not the source of any safety failure described in Part 3 §1. They should be treated as load-bearing structure: do not touch them unless a specific fix requires it, and document any such touch as a deliberate change to a verified component.

**The defects are in the seams, not the motor.** Specifically, in three seams:

1. **The config↔solver seam.** The configuration declares hard antagonisms; the solver implements them as soft. The configuration declares an objective-weights file; the solver ignores it. The seam between "what the config says" and "what the solver does" is not verified, and the divergence is invisible.
2. **The data↔schema seam.** The data file declares itself schema-conformant; the schema declares the data non-conformant (21 errors); no gate connects the two. The schema for the LP parameters file expects fields the data does not contain (`breed`, `domains`); no gate catches the mismatch. The seam between "what the data is" and "what the schema says it should be" is not verified.
3. **The solution↔output seam.** The solver computes a real solution with real nutrient values; the output layer reports `"adequate"` for everything regardless. The seam between "what the solver computed" and "what the output says" is not verified.

Each of these seams is the seam-version of the structural pattern in §3.1: a contract that exists in name only. The remedy is the same: introduce a verification at each seam (Task B1 at the solution↔output seam, Tasks B2a/B6 at the config↔solver and data↔schema seams, Task B7 across all three), and the seams become honest. The motor stays.

### §4.1 The One Piece That Comes Closest to Redesign: The Nutrient Registry

The nutrient registry is the one piece of the system that approaches "needs redesign" rather than "needs remediation." As described in §3.2, the three competing naming schemes with zero overlap and no canonical reference make the data model fragile in a way that no amount of point-fixing can resolve. The fix is Task B7: introduce a single canonical registry with `id + unit + basis` for every nutrient, and migrate every other module to reference nutrients by registry ID. This is correctly classified as a structural high-value task in the unified map (§5.4), not a blocker — the surgical fixes can land without it, but the system remains fragile until B7 ships.

It is important to be precise about what "redesign" means here. It does not mean "rewrite the LP from scratch" — the LP formulation stays. It does not mean "rewrite the solver" — the cascade stays. It means "introduce one new canonical data structure (the registry) and migrate the references." That is a meaningful piece of work, but it is bounded and surgical-to-the-data-model, not architectural-to-the-system.

### §4.2 The Speculative Refactors That Are Deliberately Deferred

The diagnosis and remediation plan identified several refactors that would improve the codebase but do not address any defect and do not threaten safety or correctness. These are deliberately deferred under the YAGNI (You Aren't Gonna Need It) and DTSTTCPW (Do The Simplest Thing That Could Possibly Work) principles, on the grounds that effort spent on them is effort not spent on the safety-critical surface, and that none of them is load-bearing for any remediation task. They are listed here for completeness and to make the deferral explicit:

- The **1,661-line `solver.py` god module** (`build_lp_problem` alone is 474 lines) — refactor to split into smaller modules. Deferred: the size is a maintenance hazard, not a correctness or safety hazard. (Part 1 §A.E12 / F-ARCH-4.)
- The **`core.py` grab-bag module** mixing infrastructure, domain, and documentation concerns — refactor to split along concern boundaries. Deferred: same reason. (Part 1 §A.E9 / F-ARCH-1.)
- The **type-model split across two modules** ("to avoid circular imports") and the pervasive `TypedDict(total=False)` pattern that provides no runtime enforcement — consolidate into a single enforced type model. Deferred: the type holes do not currently produce any safety failure. (Part 1 §A.E10, §A.E13 / F-ARCH-2, F-TYPE-1.)
- The **42% of the package that is doc-generation machinery** — simplify or remove. Deferred: doc-gen code is maintenance overhead, not a safety risk. (Part 1 §A.E11 / F-ARCH-3.)
- The **mojibake in 17 of 28 ingredient display names** (double-encoded UTF-8) — fix the encoding. Deferred: cosmetic; does not affect the LP or the safety surface. (Part 1 §A.C16 / DATA-F16.)
- **Continuous mutation testing** — add to CI. Deferred: useful for catching regressions, not for fixing existing defects.
- A **second solver** (e.g., HiGHS or OR-Tools) for diff-testing against CBC — add. Deferred: useful for catching solver-specific bugs, not for fixing any current defect.
- **Property-based tests** (Hypothesis) — add. Deferred: useful for edge-case discovery, not for the current safety surface.

Each of these is acknowledged as a real improvement and explicitly placed in the backlog (Part 2 §7, P2/P3 debt). None is in the unified execution map's active lanes (§5.1–§5.4) — they are listed in §5.5 (explicitly discarded or deferred) so that the boundary between "in scope for the current remediation" and "in the backlog" is unambiguous.

### §5.2 The Real Safety Chain — What Actually Protects the Dog Today

Because Level 1 is structurally unreachable (§3.3), the diets the user actually receives are Level-2 (or, in worse cases, Level-3) solutions. Level 2 permits antagonism violations; Level 3 relaxes inclusion constraints. The protection that exists at these levels, today, is **nothing** — the recommendation engine always emits `SAFE_TO_FEED` regardless of severity. The two tasks below are the ones that actually protect the dog once they land. They are not blockers in the G3 sense (they can begin immediately), but they are the **decisive** tasks for safety: nothing else in the program produces a user-visible distinction between a safe diet and a dangerous one.

| Task | What it is | Why it is the decisive protection |
|---|---|---|
| **B1** | Fix the hardcoded `nutrient_results` placeholder: compute real per-nutrient values, real `pct_of_min` and `pct_of_sul`, real `status` (adequate / marginal / deficient / excessive) from the LP solution. | This is the fix for the A3 defect — the "fake output" half of the §1 verdict. After B1, the user sees the truth. Until B1, every output is a lie. |
| **B2b** | Severity-scaled feeding recommendation: the recommendation engine must distinguish "1.5% over the Ca maximum" from "631% over the Ca:Mg range" and emit graduated guidance (`SAFE_TO_FEED` / `CAUTION` / `DO_NOT_FEED`) accordingly. | This is the decisive protection because Level 1 is unreachable — the user always receives a Level-2 solution, and B2b is the only mechanism that distinguishes a slightly-off Level-2 solution from a catastrophically-off one. Without B2b, B1 just shows the user the bad numbers under a `SAFE_TO_FEED` label; with B2b, the label itself reflects the severity. |

Note that B2b's *thresholds* (the exact numerical severity cutoffs) are G3-dependent (see §5.3) — the *mechanism* (graduated recommendation based on computed slack) is not. The recommended sequencing is: ship B2b's mechanism with conservative placeholder thresholds, then tighten the thresholds once G3 lands. This delivers user-visible protection immediately without waiting on the veterinary review.

### §5.3 G3-Dependent — Need Primary Sources + Veterinary Review

## §6. The Real Bottleneck

The unified execution map (§5) makes the bottleneck visible at a glance: **G3 is the only item in the entire remediation program that lies outside engineering control.** Everything else — every blocker, every safety-chain task, every structural high-value task, every surgical fix — can begin the moment work starts. None of them waits on a decision, on a review, or on a primary-source lookup.

The G3 gate requires three things, in increasing order of difficulty:

1. **Primary-source lookup.** The correct numerical values for absolute Ca and P ceilings, the growth-energy curve, the trace-mineral SULs, and the severity thresholds must be retrieved from AAFCO 2024 (dog food nutrient profiles), NRC 2006 (Nutrient Requirements of Dogs and Cats), and FEDIAF 2024 (Nutritional Guidelines for Complete and Complementary Pet Food for Cats and Dogs). These are published documents; the lookup is bounded work.
2. **Breed-specific adjustment.** German Shepherd Dogs are a large breed, but they are not the largest; the growth-curve and Ca/P numbers must be adjusted for the breed's adult weight range and growth trajectory, not just lifted from a generic large-breed table.
3. **Veterinary review.** Ideally, the final numbers should be reviewed by a board-certified veterinary nutritionist (DACVN or ECVCN). This is the genuinely slow step: it requires finding a nutritionist, scheduling a review, and incorporating their feedback. It is the only step in the entire program that depends on a person outside the engineering team.

While G3 is in flight, the unified map's G3-independent tasks (§5.1, §5.2, §5.4) deliver the largest safety-and-truth gain for the least effort, and they do not wait on anyone. The recommended sequencing is therefore:

1. **Immediately:** B0 (safety freeze), B5 (restore `_shared.py`), B6 (CI schema gate), B11 (Level-1 diagnosis), B2a (harden antagonisms), G2 (delete `objective_weights.json`). These are the blockers.
2. **In parallel with the above:** B1 (real reporting layer), B2b (severity-scaled recommendation, mechanism with placeholder thresholds). These are the safety chain.
3. **In parallel with G3:** B7 (canonical nutrient registry), B12 (reframed arginine confirmation), C5/C9 (schema fixes). These are the structural high-value tasks.
4. **After G3 lands:** B3 (absolute Ca/P ceilings), B4 (growth-energy curve), B2b (severity thresholds tightened to validated values).

The bottom line on the bottleneck is that **the project is not blocked.** It is gated, at exactly one point, on a non-engineering input. Every other workstream can and should proceed.

## §8. Task B12 Reframed (Arginine)

The original B12 task was framed as "relocate `arginine_g` from a top-level key in `bromatological_profile` into `bp["nutrients"]`, so that the Lys:Arg ratio can be evaluated." Direct inspection of the live data and code establishes that **this relocation is unnecessary: arginine is already correctly placed.**

The verification, performed against the live repository, found the following:

- **`arginine_g` is already in `bp["nutrients"]` for all 28 ingredients.** A search for any top-level `arginine_g` key in any `bromatological_profile` returned zero occurrences. The original framing of B12 described a data-model state that does not exist in the repository.
- **`arginine_g` is already in `NUTRIENT_REGISTRY`** (`lp_parameters_data.json`, line 20). The registry entry exists and is correctly typed.
- **`arginine_g` already has its own AAFCO minimum constraint** in `constraints.json` (`arginine_g >= 2.5`, energy-normalized to per-1000-kcal basis). The constraint is present and active.
- **`build_matrix()` carries arginine into the LP matrix with the correct, non-zero value.** Direct execution confirmed the values: `beef_muscle_raw → 6.86`, `chicken_muscle_raw → 11.94` (energy-normalized). The LP sees arginine.
- **The Lys:Arg constraint is built with the correct value.** The constraint `1.0 * arginine_g <= lysine_g <= 1.4 * arginine_g` is constructed using the real arginine value from the matrix. The LP internally respects Lys:Arg.

The LP therefore correctly handles arginine and Lys:Arg. The data model is correct. The constraint is correct. The matrix builder is correct. **The only thing that is broken is the reporting layer (A3).** Here is the mechanism: `arginine_g` is not among the 17 nutrients listed as scenario targets in `scenarios.json`. When the output layer builds `nutrient_results`, it iterates over the scenario targets and looks up each one in `targets_per_day`. For `arginine_g`, `targets_per_day.get(nid, 0)` falls through to the default `0` — and so the output reports `arginine_g` with `value=0` and `status="adequate"` (the hardcoded status from A3), even though the solver actually computed a real, non-zero arginine value and the Lys:Arg constraint was satisfied.

This is not an arginine-specific problem; it is exactly the A3 mechanism. Any nutrient not in the scenario-target list will be reported with `value=0` and `status="adequate"` regardless of what the solver computed. **The arginine case is a symptom of A3, not a separate defect.**

The implication for the unified map is that Task B12 should be reframed as follows:

> **Task B12 (reframed):** Confirm that fixing A3 (Task B1, the real reporting layer) also correctly displays arginine. Specifically, verify that after B1 ships, `arginine_g` appears in `nutrient_results` with its real computed value and a `status` derived from the actual Lys:Arg constraint satisfaction, rather than the current `value=0` / `status="adequate"` placeholder. If B1 alone does not produce this — for example, because `arginine_g` is not added to the scenario-target list as part of B1 — then a small follow-up to add `arginine_g` (and any other Lys:Arg-relevant amino acid) to the scenario targets is required. **No relocation of the arginine key in `bromatological_profile` is required.**

This reframing does not change the health of the system. The LP already respects Lys:Arg internally; the constraint is enforced; the data model is correct. The reframing only avoids spending engineering effort on a relocation that resolves a non-existent problem, and ensures that the arginine display is correctly fixed by B1 (or by a minimal B1-follow-up) rather than by a redundant B12.
